# Baseline recording script

Read this on your phone while you record. It takes about 6–8 minutes.

## Setup (keep it the same every time so re-recordings compare fairly)
- Use a quiet room with soft furnishings (a bedroom is better than a bathroom or kitchen). Turn off fans and the aircon.
- Hold the phone about **20 cm (one hand-span) from your mouth**, a little off to the side so you don't blow on the mic.
- **iPhone:** Voice Memos, with Settings → Voice Memos → Audio Quality → **Lossless**.
  **Android:** the built-in Recorder, or "ASR Voice Recorder" set to WAV.
- Record **one file per section**. Use the file names below and drop the files in `recordings/baseline/`.
- **Use your normal everyday voice.** Don't try to sound femme. We need to measure where you are now.
- Drink some water first. Stop if anything hurts or tickles.

## 1. `01_hvd.m4a`: hVd words (these match the Cox et al. norms)
Read the list **three times**, slowly, with a short pause after each word and a falling "list" tone:

> heed · hid · head · had · hard · hud · hod · hoard · hood · who'd · heard · hay · high · hoy · how · hoe · here · hair

## 2. `02_sustained.m4a`: sustained vowels
At a comfortable pitch and normal loudness, hold each vowel for about **4 seconds**. Breathe between them. Do the whole set twice.

> "ahh" (as in *hard*) · "eee" (as in *heed*) · "ooo" (as in *who'd*)

## 3. `03_glide.m4a`: pitch range (gentle!)
- Slide from your **lowest comfortable** note up to your **highest comfortable** note on "ooo", then back down, like a lazy siren. Do it 3 times.
- **No straining and no pushing for the top.** If it cracks into falsetto, that's fine; just let it happen.

## 4. `04_stella.m4a`: "Please Call Stella"
Read it twice at your normal speed:

> Please call Stella. Ask her to bring these things with her from the store: six spoons of fresh snow peas, five thick slabs of blue cheese, and maybe a snack for her brother Bob. We also need a small plastic snake and a big toy frog for the kids. She can scoop these things into three red bags, and we will go meet her Wednesday at the train station.

## 5. `05_free.m4a`: free talking (30–60 s)
Talk casually, as if you were on the phone to a mate. Pick one:
- what you did last weekend
- a meal you'd cook for someone
- the worst job you've ever had

## 6. (Optional, very useful) `06_attempt.m4a`: your current "femme try"
Read the Stella passage once more in whatever version of a lighter or more femme voice you can do right now. Go only as far as is comfortable. This shows me what you can already reach.

# Reference clips → `recordings/reference/`
- Use **3–4 clips per speaker, each 10–30 s**, with **one voice only**: no music, laughter or crosstalk. Interviews and podcasts are ideal.
- Name the files by speaker, for example `hunter_01.wav` or `aus_f_<name>_01.wav`.
- Hunter Schafer: interview clips. These are for **gender features only** (pitch, resonance, weight, intonation). We'll ignore her vowels.
- 2–3 Australian women in general to cultivated speech, for the **vowel and accent** targets. Good sources are ABC presenters, newsreaders and interviewees, or actors talking in interviews (not in roles).
- These clips are for your private analysis only. Don't commit them anywhere public.

# Extra baseline recordings (record these before you start practising, in WAV)
These go in `recordings/baseline/` too. Use WAV: either record inside Lilt and export, or use ASR Voice Recorder set to WAV. The built-in recorder compresses away detail above about 4 kHz.

## 7. `07_babble.wav`: loud condition
Play café or party noise from a **second device** (a laptop or speaker, not headphones), loud enough that you'd naturally raise your voice. That's about 70 dB, or a busy café. Then read "Please Call Stella" once, as you would to someone across a café table. Don't strain.

## 8. `08_steps.wav`: pitch steps
Say "one, two, three" at your normal pitch. Then say it again "a bit higher". Do that three times, so there are four steps in total. Don't plan the steps; just do what feels like "a bit higher".
