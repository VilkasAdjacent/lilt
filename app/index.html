    Headphones or speaker?

    One earbud in works best. The mic then hears only you, so the readings stay sharp, and your other ear can still hear your own voice. With both ears covered, everyone pushes harder.

    On the phone speaker the mic hears the café too, and this app doesn't filter it out. Practice still works, but loudness, weight and F2 get rougher, and I'll mark those tries with ≈. Keep the volume moderate: it only needs to feel busy.

      About & voice safety

      About Lilt

      What it is

      A little practice buddy that shows your pitch, a guess at vocal weight, loudness and resonance (formants) while you talk. It's built around one goal: getting louder without getting heavier.

      Privacy

      Your audio never leaves this device. No server, no uploads, no analytics. Recordings are kept in this browser's storage until you delete them. Clearing site data deletes them too.

      Voice care 💧

      • Short and often beats long and heroic. 5 to 15 minutes is plenty. Lilt will nudge you around the 12-minute mark.
      • Stop if it hurts, tickles, gets scratchy or tired, or your throat feels tight. Rest is part of practice.
      • Loud should come from breath and space, not squeeze. If getting louder feels like pushing in your throat, back off a notch.
      • Sip water. Yawn-sighs and lip trills are lovely warm-ups and cool-downs.
      • Hoarse for more than about two weeks, or any pain or a lost voice? See a GP or ENT. A speech pathologist who works with trans voices is gold if you can get to one.

      About the numbers

      • Pitch is fairly trustworthy (within a few cents on clean tones).
      • Weight is a rough guess from H1–H2 and spectral tilt. It moves with vowel and mic too. Use it to compare yourself today with yourself a moment ago, not with anyone else.
      • Formants (F1–F3) come from a classic LPC method. They can wobble on high pitches or on "ee"/"oo" vowels.
      • Loudness is dBFS, which is relative to your mic, not real-world decibels.

      None of these is a grade. Your voice is yours on every day, at every number.

      If things feel heavy

      Voice work can stir stuff up. If you're having a rough time, you deserve support too. In Australia: Lifeline 13 11 14, QLife (LGBTIQ+ peer support) 1800 184 527.
